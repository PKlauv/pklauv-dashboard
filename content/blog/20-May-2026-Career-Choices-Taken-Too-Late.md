> Some say, "Don't dwell on the past." To that I say:
>
> I am trying to learn from it, so that I can move forward, with possibly more knowledge and reflection ability to make better life choices.


When I was younger, I was never the studious type. I didn't see the point of it, it wasn't worth it to me. I was focused on playing football and playing online games with my friends. I never thought I'd end up where I am now: **wanting to do more.**

I'm done with my second year now, two of the four for my bachelor's, and it's safe to say I regret some of the decisions I've made along the way. Not in a bitter way, more like I can finally see the shape of them.
So let me start from the actual start.

At primary school I was all good, playing football, not majorly into school but still did well. 
Then I was rather fortunate enough to go to a private middle school focusing in sports. I had to travel far to school, and wasn't able to properly connect with friends and groups in a way I'd hoped to. 
I then fell into a world of gaming, which I would spend thousands of hours doing. And that's where the **real falling behind** in school started. 

After finishing middle school, I wasn't really happy with where I was at, and what the sports high school was about. 
I decided I wanted to go back to my local school, meet my friends again and have it all be the way it was.

That's what I *thought*. 

The reality of it was that I'd been away for too long, and struggled to get to know them again after 3 years of not talking regularly. 

**This was my fault.** I couldn't communicate properly. I had been in my gaming bubble for too long. Didn't really fully escape it until 3 years after, which was when I finished high school. 

Even had some therapy sessions because of the gaming, which, looking back, is wild to me.

Some choices I made while I was starting high school included starting the "Realfag" route, which is basically taking these heavy classes, normally being harder math, physics, chemistry, or something along those lines. 
I wanted to do that, I wanted to take the "realfag" route, simply because I didn't know anything else, I didn't put enough time or effort to look into my future and/or present. 

I ended up taking some level of math (not the hardest one) and geography courses. Which would still barely satisfy the requirement. 
So I did that, and ended up with below par grades and didn't have a clue what I was going to end up doing. 

My sister had travelled to university in the US two years prior, and I realized I could do the same, somewhere I could go, since I could play sports at a decent level. So I saw college in the US as an easy escape, and I didn't look much into what the schools stood for, or what I'd spend the next four years studying. 
My thought process was simply this:

- "What have I come here for?"  ->  To play football while getting a degree.
- "What am I good at in my spare time?"  ->  Gaming... and computers, I guess.

And that turned into playing soccer at Gannon University, and majoring in Computer Science.
I've been there two years now, and I've learnt a great deal since leaving to study abroad.

This isn't a pity story, this is a learning story, and I'd like to share some valuable lessons that I keep close and take with me in the future.

### Lesson 1 — The best days are determined by the mornings

I've realized the days I can't get up start slow and stay unproductive. The days I do the opposite — up on the first alarm, early if I can — go the other way. You make your bed, then do the next good thing, then the next. **All because you got up.**

### Lesson 2 — Gaming got me nothing

Don't take this the wrong way. A lot of good can come from gaming, I enjoyed my time while gaming, and I made friends while gaming, some of which I am still in contact with today.

But the truth is, that when looking back at the many hours and days I spent playing the game, none of it has got me where I am and where I need to be. In my personal experience, which is not everyone's experience, it's been a *massive waste of time*.

Set yourself up first → get a job → do your homework → then spend that time however you'd like. But **do not** prioritize gaming over other necessary things.

### Lesson 3 — Don't let your future control you

My best tip is to spend actual hours and actual time with big decisions on your future. As someone who didn't myself, I've almost felt the future *just happen*, instead of taking it by the palm, and having a real goal, and reaching that goal.

Spend some time finding out what you really want to do with life, and as I've experienced, recruiters want someone that's passionate about their subject, not someone who chose their profession for money (in some cases that might work too though).


### Lesson 4 — Surround yourself appropriately

Surround yourself with people who have high, similar goals. Why? When I started middle school I lived far from everyone else and never connected properly. I ended up escaping into video games, spending endless hours at the PC. That snowballed — I fell behind in every part of school and didn't recover until it was too late.

What I take from it: if I'd instead surrounded myself with people who wanted to do well and keep learning, rather than disappearing into a game and avoiding everyone, **my life would've looked very different.** This shaped me through both middle and high school.

I am happy with where I am at, and look positive towards the future, even when it can be tough. I continue to educate myself and be a better person. 
Studying abroad has given me, and will keep giving me, real insight into how the world operates. And as a computer science student in particular, there's nothing better than living in a country that keeps pushing me, one of the most technologically driven places on earth.
This has been my story, and I hope you can learn from it.
